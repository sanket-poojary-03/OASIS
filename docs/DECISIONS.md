# Decisions

## 2026-11-01
- The LLM writes a validated recipe of relations, never coordinates or code (competitors that let the LLM write coordinates suffer overlaps and objects in water).
- Terrain and placement run in the browser (real-time streaming); AI and model work run in Python.
- Endless chunked noise terrain plus a 1024 m authored core.

## 2026-11-02
- Final review fix (m1, revises Task 26 I2): uvicorn no longer trusts X-Forwarded-For (`--proxy-headers --forwarded-allow-ips='*'` and `FORWARDED_ALLOW_IPS=*` are gone from the Dockerfile). In '*' mode uvicorn took the leftmost entry, which the client writes itself, so a fake header bought a fresh per-IP budget. `app/clientip.py` now gives every limiter the rightmost entry that is not a proxy hop, falling back to the socket peer. It skips private/loopback addresses and Cloudflare's published ranges as well as the bare rightmost entry, because Render's edge is Cloudflare. Forum reports say Render appends to the header without clearing it, and that the hop before the last is the client. If the rightmost entry is a Cloudflare edge, taking it blindly would put every visitor in one bucket. A client-written entry is only ever read when everything to its right is a proxy hop.

## 2026-11-03
- **AI only: no keyword world behind a working backend.** Local testing showed OpenRouter refusing about 1 in 3 calls with 429 (`gemini-2.5-flash-lite` "temporarily rate-limited upstream", shared Google pool; key and credit fine), and the backend silently answered with the keyword mock, so players saw fake "AI" worlds. Now: `LLMClient` retries 408/429/5xx/timeouts with a short wait, then each `LLM_FALLBACK_MODELS` model (default `google/gemini-2.5-flash`); if all fail the API returns 503 "The AI is busy right now…" and the browser shows it and keeps the world unchanged. The browser's offline mode is used only when the backend cannot be reached at all (fetch fails), and is labelled offline. The backend's keyword mock remains only when no `LLM_API_KEY` is set (labelled mock). Revises the I4 "busy → offline" toast.
- **Online models: one whole object, small.** "cafe" fetched a 17 MB textured coffee-shop interior scene (the LLM picker saw only titles and chose the first). Now: titles naming an interior/room/scene/diorama/environment are skipped unless the player asked for one; online downloads are capped at 8 MB (`ONLINE_MAX_DOWNLOAD`) and a failed or oversized download gives way to the next candidate; the picker prompt asks for one object seen from outside and the lower triangle count on a tie, and it gets one try per model so a busy AI never stalls model loading.
- **Every LLM request caps its answer (`max_tokens`, `LLM_MAX_TOKENS` default 4000; picker 100).** OpenRouter reserves the cost of the longest possible answer, so uncapped calls on a low balance returned 402 (the backup model failed this way in testing). A full 12-place recipe is well under 3000 tokens.
- **Doors follow the model.** The stand-in door slab was sized once from the placeholder and stayed put when a loaded model replaced it, so it floated outside the real house (and lamp glow height / push bodies kept placeholder sizes too). Now `WorldObjects` fires `onReady` again after a model swap and `InteractionSystem.register` replaces the old set-up: placeholders keep the stand-in slab; a loaded model with a part named like a door (`findDoorNode`, not frame/way/handle…) has that part hinged on its edge (`hingeDoor`) and swung by E; a model without one has no door interaction. Online Poly Pizza models are single merged meshes, so their houses are scenery; pack prefabs with separate door parts swing.
- **Bloom is night-only.** Facing the sun, the daytime bloom (strength 0.25, threshold 0.85, applied before tone mapping) picked up the HDR sky near the sun and smeared a white-blue haze over houses and trees (screenshots with bloom on/off confirmed it). `bloomFor(night)`: off in daylight, strength 0.9×night and threshold 0.85→0.55 through dusk and night, which is all the windows, lamps and fires need. Also saves a full-screen pass by day.
- **No refusals for ordinary places.** flash-lite answered `unsupported` to "a big modern city" despite the prompt rule. The world prompt now welcomes cities/modern/futuristic places (a city = large settlement + landmarks/structures) and the service questions one refusal ("unsupported is only for harmful requests"; does not use up the invalid-answer retry); a second refusal stands. ## 2026-11-07 (later): Floating models, invisible walls, a resolution choice
- **The floating cart: three's default bounds.** `Box3.setFromObject` turns each mesh's own box and boxes that, so a model tipped inside its file measures far bigger than it is. Quaternius' Broken Cart measured 3.2 m tall and was drawn about 1.4 m tall, hanging 1.22 m above the ground. `drawnBox` reads the vertices, and `fitToHeight`, `fitWidth`, `widenHouse` and `setContent` use it. Fitted correctly, that cart was 7.7 m long, so scattered props are capped at 5 m wide (`EXTRA_MAX_WIDTH`; cars and named props keep theirs).
- **Slopes: `world/groundPose.ts`.** Objects stood at the height under their middle, so the low side hung (a fallen log by 0.7 m). The model's base (its lowest 15%, `baseOf`, so sails and sign boards do not count) is now sampled against the ground. A low prop leans to the slope (at most about 27°) if it is no taller than twice its base and no wider than 8 m. Anything else stays upright and sinks until its low side touches, at most a fifth of its height and 1.5 m. Skipped for pushed props and cars, which have their own bodies, and for anything over 0.25 m from the ground, which stands on something. Scattered rocks, logs, stumps, bushes and pebbles sink by slope × reach, at most 0.5 m. Checked: no whole object floats among 141 in the fishing valley or 261 in the menu world. This counts a model only when all of it is off the ground; part of a model can still overhang a dip.
- **Invisible walls had three causes, all gone.** (1) Loaded while far away: the range culler had hidden the root, so `surfaceOf`, which read only visible objects, found nothing and the object got a box. That was 78 of 261 objects on the menu world, and is now 0. (2) Detailed group buildings collided as a convex hull. (3) Rigged or over-20k-triangle models got a box. Now everything uses the model's own shape, simplified by vertex clustering to at most 1,500 triangles (`simplifySurface`). Measured at chest height against the exact mesh, on the menu's models: the hull stuck out 0.2–0.8 m on average and up to 2 m, the box up to 5 m, the simplified mesh about 0 m on average, with 0.23 m at worst on the tavern and 1.41 m on one watermill ray. Cost while pressing against a tavern: exact mesh 1.06 ms a call, simplified 0.43, hull 0.35. Rebuilding all 261 colliders takes 161 ms. Walking through a model is now possible only where its own shape has a gap wider than the player.
- **A narrower player.** The capsule was 0.35 m in radius with a 5 cm skin, so you stopped 0.4 m short of everything. Now 0.28 and 2 cm, the same 1.8 m tall. `FEET` is 0.92, measured at rest on flat ground.
- **Resolution menu (button, or R).** Auto is the existing `RenderScale`, and it stays the default so slow GPUs keep their frame rate. Low, Medium, High and Ultra are 0.5, 0.75, 1 and the screen's own ratio (1.5 to 2). A fixed choice is never changed by the frame rate, and is saved in `localStorage`. The list shows real pixel sizes, because "High" is 1 pixel per CSS pixel, which is below native on a scaled screen: integrated GPUs are capped at 1 under Auto, so on a 150% screen only Ultra is native.

## 2026-11-07: Water reaches its shores
Reported with a screenshot: a straight-edged beige wedge in a river, gaps and sharp cutoffs in the water, and land where water should run. Reproduced in the browser at each cause, then measured on 40 test worlds.
- **Rivers and lakes are one sheet on the terrain's grid (`world/waterSheet.ts`), not a ribbon and discs.** The ground's channel is shaped by distance from the river's line, and an offset ribbon cannot follow that. Cutting it into runs for culling made each run's end normals disagree, so every bend had a wedge with its base on the bank and its tip mid-river (the screenshot). It also folded over itself (dark streaks from doubled transparency) and stopped square across the channel's round source (about 300 m² of dry pit per river). The sheet uses the terrain's vertices and its triangle split, so the shore is exactly where the two surfaces cross, and past the shore it dives 0.3 m under the ground, so an edge cannot show. Pieces are per chunk, for `RangeCuller`.
- **Land under the sea stays under it.** River banks were forced to sea level + 0.4 m wherever a river ran, which built sand causeways across open water: up to 11,376 m² raised out of the sea in one test world. Bank height now fades out at the sea's level, and a valley's river keeps only the stretch between its shores (`longestRun`), so a flooded valley is simply a sea inlet. River points over the sea are pinned to the sea's level.
- **A lake's level comes from the land around it, not the land under it.** Its water stands 0.2 m below the lowest ground out to 1.4 radii, so the land holds it with no embankment, and in a world with a sea it cannot sit in a raised ring on the shore. A lake with no river now prefers low, level ground (smallest highest point), and tries radii 45, 30, 20 until one fits.
- **No sill at a lake's mouth.** The channel was skipped inside a lake's radius, so the lake's shore rim dammed the river, up to 0.13 m above the water. The channel now cuts through it.
- **A river's mouth has no colour line.** The river's ripple material and the sea's `Water.js` cannot be made to match, so river water fades out over its last 1.5 m above the sea and the sea shows through. Sheet triangles at or below the sea are not drawn at all (they flickered against it). The sea seen from below keeps its own, unfaded copy of the material.
- **Traced rivers are smoothed (`smoothLine`).** They turn in 22.5° steps, which drew long straight banks that met in spikes.
- **Cost:** `waterSheet` takes up to about 150 ms on a 1 km river, once per world, on the main thread (first-run timing under vitest). `createField` up to about 37 ms.
- **Not changed:** rivers keep a constant width and lakes are still round, so shores are smooth but regular. A valley's river ends in a rounded pool at the foot of the map-edge mountains. `field.ts` is 439 lines, past the 400 guideline (it was 406). `menu-poster.webp` was not retaken: the start screen's village and river are still in the same places.

## 2026-11-07: Roads stay off the water
Reported from the demo: roads drawn on top of the water. Reproduced with the demo's own prompt: four crossings, 138 m of dirt ribbon, two of them at one spot.
- **A crossing is a real bridge, drawn apart from the road (`render/roads.ts`).** A wooden deck that arches from bank to bank, posts into the bed, and rails. It was only the road's own ribbon raised 1.2 m. Plain road is no longer drawn over water at all. This keeps a world's places joined: the alternative of no crossings cuts every place across a river off its road network. **The user's report said roads over water "should not be there"; a bridge is my reading of what should replace them. Say if there should be no crossing at all.**
- **Crossing is priced by the metre (`WATER_COST`), not per cell.** A flat price per lattice cell made a slant as cheap as going straight, since a diagonal step covers more ground for the same price. The land beside water costs extra too (`SHORE_COST`), so roads keep back from the bank.
- **A span is one straight segment, trimmed to the real banks (`layOut`).** Smoothing the whole path drew crossings as long slants. Spans now run 33–37 m over a 32 m channel, where lattice-to-lattice spans reached 56 m.
- **The network prices water as well (`roadNetwork(nodes, extra, price)`).** It chose roads by distance alone, so every road near a river crossed it. Crossing now costs an extra 600 m, so a river is usually crossed once. Paths the AI asks for by name are still built.
- **A road's edge counts (`isClearOfWater`).** Roads and streets tested only their centre line against water, so an edge could hang over the bank.
- **A road near a river hugs the ground.** `roadY` lifted anything within a river's reach by 1.2 m, even on dry bank.
- **Not changed:** cars still stop at water, so they cannot cross a bridge (`vehicles.ts`). An ocean world's boardwalks are as they were: they are its streets. A bridge has no collider on its rails.

## 2026-11-08: Roads and canyons
Reported from a live Mars world: a road ran down the canyon wall and a bridge sat low between the cliffs. It should span from rim to rim, with the road following it. Bridges existed only over water, so a road had to go down to the river, cross at river level and climb out.
- **A high span is a move in the road search (`findPath`, `spansGap`), not a repair afterwards.** The search weighs it against any way round. It starts from a lip and lands on the first level spot past the gap, and must stay under `MAX_SPAN` (170 m; a canyon is about 140 m rim to rim, and `MAX_BRIDGE` still limits crossings of water).
- **What counts as a gap:** at least 8 m deep measured to the water, not the bed; walls too steep for a road somewhere under it; about level ends; ground under the deck nowhere; no sea (islets stay apart). The first version measured to the bed and had no wall rule, and it turned a 34 m river bridge in `fishing-valley` into a 79 m viaduct, 60% slower to build. `roadsOverCanyon.test.ts` now holds that bridge to its size.
- **Cliffs stop roads, in two tiers.** `CLIFF` (0.9) first. Only where that finds no way does a road take up to `WALL` (2.0), and never if it dips over 25 m below both ends. Measured: mountain worlds need up to 1.8 to reach every place, and slope alone cannot tell them from low canyons (walls 1.2 and up), but a mountain road climbs to where it is going while a gorge road plunges and climbs out. A place on the floor of a sheer canyon gets no road, and the log says so.
- **A city's own streets stop at cliffs too (`street`).** They are straight lines over the city's grid, broken only at water, and they ran off the rim at grades of 6 to 9: the dark stripes on the canyon wall.
- **The network prices cliffs as it prices water (`crossesBarrier`),** so the canyon is crossed once, not by every road near it.
- **A bridge carries cars (`deckSurface`, `VehicleEnv.deckAt`).** Cars take their height from the terrain, so they would have fallen through a dry span. It answers by height, so a car under a span stays on the ground. This also ends the old limit that cars stopped at water and could not use a bridge. In headless Chrome a car crossed an 89 m span at 108 km/h at deck height, about 77 m over the ground.
- **Tall spans stand on concrete piers with a girder (`bridgeFrame`).** Thin posts every 6 m read as toothpicks at this height; low bridges keep their posts. A paved road keeps its asphalt on the deck.
- **Checked:** two live worlds, on `sarvam`/`glm5.3-flash`. "A Mars city built along a deep red canyon, with districts on both sides": 107 m spans, deck at 75 m over ground at 19 m, steepest road 0.2, every place reached. "A city inspired by Mars": the AI chose plains, so no bridge was needed.
- **Not changed:** the AI's choice of terrain, and placement (a settlement can still choose a canyon floor). A span has no collider on its rails, so the player can walk off the side.

## 2026-11-08: Recipe flora: the AI names what grows

- **An optional `flora` list (at most 8) and a `setFlora` op, in both schemas.** Each entry is `{asset, layer, density, spot, height?}`. Before this, the LLM could only set temperature and moisture, and fixed `scatterRules` picked from 17 kinds: a "haunted swamp" grew oaks and an "alien planet" grew oaks on purple ground. The brief already described vegetation; now it has somewhere to go.
- **The AI says which plants, code still says where and how many.** A named plant replaces the preset kinds of its *layer* (canopy: trees, logs, stumps; shrub: bushes, cacti; ground: flowers, ferns, mushrooms, reeds; rock: rocks, pebbles). Its chance per cell is `LAYER_BASE × LAND_SHARE[biome] × density`, so the biome still shapes the world: thick in forests, thin on meadows, rare in deserts. Cliffs keep their preset rocks, riverbeds their reeds. A layer the AI did not name keeps its preset plants. `nature` still scales every layer.
- **A spot is where a plant thrives, not the only place it grows.** In the first live test the swamp's trees were all `wet`, and a hard filter left every cell away from water with no trees at all (the preset trees were gone too). Now a suited plant wins over its layer's `anywhere` plants, and where no plant of a layer is suited they all grow at a quarter (`OFF_SPOT`).
- **Several plants on one layer grow in patches** picked by a slow noise per layer (as oak, birch and autumn groves already were), not mixed like salt.
- **Library only, like the base art (`loadFlora`).** Scatter draws thousands of copies, and online models are up to 30k faces and a different style. The library already had 56 plant models, many never used (willow, 5 twisted trees, 7 alien trees, crystals, snowy bush and rock, big leafy plants); the prompt lists their names. The finder's partial matching covers near names ("glowing mushroom" → mushroom, "alder tree" → oak). A plant with no match grows as its layer's preset kind (`FLORA_FALLBACK`) and the player is told. Each plant is fetched once per page.
- **Kinds are strings now.** A named plant's kind is `layer:asset` ("canopy:twisted spooky tree"); preset kinds have no colon. Models are keyed by asset, so they carry across worlds and edits.
- **Worlds without flora are unchanged, and a test proves it.** `golden.test.ts` hashes positions, normals, colours, scatter and grass of 5 chunks in each fixture world, recorded before the change. The no-flora path runs exactly the old rules with the same random draws.
- **Slips are repaired, not rejected (`_repair_flora`).** "trees" → canopy, "stones" → rock, density clamped to 0..2, an unknown spot → anywhere, nameless entries dropped.
- **Not done (later phases):** plants found online (needs a triangle budget), regions with their own climate and colour, grass colour per region.
- **Known:** the LLM also adds flora to ordinary worlds despite the prompt. The results were sensible (willows by a river), but an unknown tree name then replaces the preset oak, birch and autumn groves with oaks alone.

## 2026-11-08: Scatter models are held to a triangle budget

- **The user's haunted swamp ran slowly. Measured, not guessed:** its plants weighed 6.04M triangles on the loaded land against 1.64M for the same world without flora (headless Chrome, all chunks loaded, instances × triangles). Draw calls were not the problem (267 against 331). Nearly all of it was two trees: the library's twisted tree is about 9,600 triangles and the dead tree about 6,000, against 3,200 to 6,300 for an oak and 1,600 to 5,000 for a pine, and the AI asked for them at density 1.4 and 1.2. Half the weight was in the 3×3 chunks around the player, which also draw the trees again for shadows, so thinning the outer rings alone could not fix it.
- **Every scatter model is simplified on load to a budget (`render/lightModel.ts`):** 4,500 triangles near (a preset oak's weight) and a second copy of 1,500 for the outer rings, which are in fog (`ScatterAssets` draws `far` at lod 1 and 2). It is meshoptimizer through three's `SimplifyModifier` (already installed, WASM), with normals and UVs weighed so shading and texture seams hold. Each mesh is simplified on its own, in proportion, so a model keeps its materials. A model under the budget is untouched. It applies to whatever model a plant gets, so no choice of the AI can make a world heavy again.
- **Result, same 3,905 plants:** the swamp 6.04M → 2.37M (−61%); the world without flora 1.64M → 1.44M (−12%), because the heaviest oaks and pines are trimmed too. Side by side at walking distance the trees are indistinguishable.
- **Not changed:** how many plants grow and where (the golden test still holds), and shadows. The swamp is still about 1.6× a preset world, because the AI asked for 25% more trees than a forest; density is the AI's to say.

## 2026-11-08: Trees are solid (trunk colliders)

- **Trees were scenery only:** scatter is instanced meshes, and the only per-chunk collider was the terrain trimesh, so the player and cars went through every tree.
- **Only the trunk is solid, an upright cylinder (`world/trunks.ts`).** You walk under the canopy and bump the trunk. A trimesh of the model would snag the player on leaves and cost a BVH per tree. Radius is 5% of the tree's drawn height (0.2 to 0.9 m), times its scale. Height is 40% of the tree, at least 2.4 m (above the player's step), with the foot sunk 0.4 m for slopes.
- **Every tree kind, preset or named (`isTreeKind`):** the seven preset trees and any `canopy:` plant the AI names. Logs and stumps stay walk-over. A named tree's trunk follows its recipe `height` (default 7 m), so a giant has a giant trunk.
- **Built with the terrain collider** in the 3×3 chunks around the player, and dropped with it (`ChunkManager.addColliders`). Cylinders are cheap beside the trimesh, so they share its per-frame budget. A flora edit rebuilds chunks, so the new trees become solid without any extra code.
- **A tree you cannot see is not solid:** with a server, a kind without its model is not drawn (`ScatterAssets.draws`), so it gets no collider. `refreshScatter` rebuilds trunks when models arrive.
- **Cars collide too, for free:** vehicles collide with everything except the player and terrain, and parking checks the same colliders, so cars no longer park inside trees.
