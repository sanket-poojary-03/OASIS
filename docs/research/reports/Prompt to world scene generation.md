# Prompts become worlds through pixels or geometry

As of November 2026, two kinds of prompt-to-world systems exist, and they do not compete directly. The first kind is **pixel-based interactive world models**: a neural network draws every frame live in response to your keyboard and mouse. Google DeepMind's Genie 3 (Aug 2025) and Genie 4 (Oct 27, 2026) lead here, alongside Odyssey, Decart, Runway GWM-1, Skywork's Matrix-Game and Tencent's HY-World 1.5. The second kind is **exportable 3D worlds**: Gaussian splats or meshes you can load into Unity, Unreal, Blender or three.js. World Labs' Marble (which AMD agreed to buy for about $8.2B) leads this group, with Tencent's open HY-World 2.0, NVIDIA's Lyra 2.0 and Meta's research-only WorldGen behind it. Next to both runs a third track, "intelligent scene generation". Here LLM and VLM agents plan layouts, write Blender or engine code, pick or generate assets, and check the result by rendering it and critiquing the images. It started with Holodeck and LayoutGPT in 2023. By 2026 it had produced physics-checked, simulation-ready scene factories (SceneSmith, NVIDIA SAGE, Tencent WorldClaw) and a large ecosystem of MCP editor bridges. blender-mcp alone has about **32.9k GitHub stars** and CoplayDev's unity-mcp about **15.6k**. What matters for a builder: pixel models make the most striking demos but export nothing you can edit, while geometry pipelines and agent-driven editors produce assets you can actually ship. The most interesting indie work on X combines the two, for example Genie frames turned into splats, Marble worlds wired into three.js games, and Claude driving Blender. The catalog below gives links to every repo, paper, product and X post the research turned up, so you can follow each one up.

### Google defines the pixel frontier but still has no developer platform

Genie 3 arrived on **Aug 5, 2025** as a real-time world model at **720p and 24 fps**. It supports "promptable world events" such as changing the weather or adding objects ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2025/08/06/15-mind-blowing-interactive-worlds-googles-genie-3-ai-can-create)). It became a paid consumer product, **Project Genie, on Jan 29, 2026** for Google AI Ultra subscribers, with sessions of about 60 seconds ([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/project-genie/)). Sources disagree on availability: Wikipedia reports a worldwide Ultra rollout in May 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Genie_(website))), while a September 2026 DataCamp guide still says US-only ([DataCamp](https://www.datacamp.com/tutorial/google-genie-3-guide)). Waymo built its robotaxi edge-case simulator on Genie 3 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waymo_World_Model)). **Genie 4 (Oct 27, 2026)** adds audio generated on the fly (ambient sound, music, dialogue) and natural-language steering of camera, weather and characters, but it is limited to trusted testers. Google published no resolution, fps or pricing ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/googles-genie-4-world-model-adds-audio-more-realistic-worlds-and-real-time-steering/)). Secondary coverage says scenes stay consistent for about two minutes, double Genie 3, but Google's own page says only "several minutes" ([Unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/google-deepmind-rolls-out-genie-4-world-model-in-research-preview/)). Genie therefore remains the quality benchmark without being something you can build products on.

Chinese labs answered each Genie release with open weights within months. Skywork's **Matrix-Game 2.0** (Aug 2025, 1.8B parameters, 25 fps) was promoted outright as an open alternative to Genie 3 ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/matrix-game-2-0-launches-as-a-powerful-open-source-alternative-to-genie-3--302527562.html)). Tencent's **HY-World 1.5 WorldPlay** followed in mid-December 2025 ([GitHub](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HY-WorldPlay)). The most architecturally interesting entry is **Matrix-Game 4.0** (Oct 2026). It puts a causal video model underneath a real game engine, so the engine handles gameplay, physics and input and the network only renders. It reaches **58–67 fps at 864×480 on RTX 50-series cards**, but ships weights for just three scenes ([GitHub](https://github.com/SkyworkAI/Matrix-Game)). This hybrid answers the main criticism of pure pixel models, that they cannot keep game state or follow rules.

The startups are funded at levels that suggest chip and cloud companies see world models as a source of compute demand. **Odyssey** raised a $310M Series B at a **$1.45B valuation** with Amazon, AMD Ventures and GV, and uses AWS Trainium ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/17/world-model-maker-odyssey-nabs-1-45b-valuation-backed-by-amazon-and-other-big-names/)). **Decart** raised $300M at about $4B with NVIDIA investing ([Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/m7yk4c4e3)); a reported Amazon acquisition at about $7B is unverified. **General Intuition**, which builds action models for agents rather than creator tools, reached **$6.2B** in September 2026 ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/29/world-model-startup-general-intuition-closes-220m-investment/)). Runway's **GWM-1** (Dec 2025) ships in Worlds, Avatars and Robotics variants ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/11/runway-releases-its-first-world-model-adds-native-audio-to-latest-video-model)). Microsoft's **WHAMM** Quake II demo showed the category's weaknesses: objects vanished and scenes changed when the player looked away ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/06/microsoft-releases-ai-generated-quake-ii-demo-but-admits-limitations)). Luma raised $900M and launched an Open Physical AI Lab but has no public world product ([Luma](https://lumalabs.ai/news/luma-open-physical-ai-lab)). A ByteDance spatial world model for Pico headsets was reported by Bloomberg but not confirmed as released ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/bytedance-spatial-video-world-model-zhang-yiming)).

### Marble is the most usable exportable-world product, now with acquisition risk

World Labs' **Marble launched commercially on Nov 12, 2025** with tiers from free (4 generations) to $95/month ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/12/fei-fei-lis-world-labs-speeds-up-the-world-model-race-with-marble-its-first-commercial-product/)). It exports high-fidelity Gaussian splats (.spz default, or .ply), low-poly collider meshes for physics, and textured GLB meshes ([World Labs blog](https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/marble-world-model)). The **World API launched Jan 21, 2026** ([World Labs blog](https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/announcing-the-world-api)), and Marble 1.1, with an auto-expanding "Plus" variant, shipped in April 2026; sources give dates from Apr 2 to Apr 8 ([NYU RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/world-labs-releases-marble-1-1-auto-expanding-3d-world-generation)). Secondary sources put API pricing at about **$1.20 per marble-1.1 world** and about $2.80 per mesh export ([invideo](https://invideo.io/blog/world-labs-marble-3d-worlds/)). World Labs also open-sourced **Spark**, an MIT three.js splat renderer whose 2.0 release adds LoD streaming ([Spark docs](https://sparkjs.dev/docs/overview/); [Spark 2.0](https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/spark-2.0)). NVIDIA documents a Marble-to-Isaac Sim robotics pipeline ([NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/simulate-robotic-environments-faster-with-nvidia-isaac-sim-and-world-labs-marble/)). On **Sept 28, 2026, AMD agreed to acquire World Labs for about $8.2B** in stock, and neither press release mentions what happens to Marble or the API ([AMD IR](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1299/amd-to-acquire-world-labs-to-advance-the-future-of-ai-compute)). Anyone building on the World API now carries vendor risk.

Tencent ships open weights in both categories. **HunyuanWorld 1.0** (July 2025) lifts a FLUX-based 360° panorama into a semantically layered mesh with separable foreground objects ([GitHub](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HunyuanWorld-1.0)). **HY-World 2.0** (code released in stages from Apr 16 to May 18, 2026) chains HY-Pano 2.0, WorldNav trajectory planning, WorldStereo 2.0 expansion and WorldMirror 2.0 reconstruction to output 3DGS, meshes and point clouds for Unity, Unreal and Isaac. Tencent says the quality is comparable to Marble ([KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/tencents-hy-world-2-0-moves-ai-beyond-video-into-editable-3d-worlds)). Its licenses are "source available", with geographic and user restrictions, not true open source ([The Neuron](https://www.theneuron.ai/explainer-articles/tencent-just-open-sourced-a-3d-world-generator)). **Meta WorldGen** comes closest to a game-ready result: an LLM-planned procedural blockout plus navmesh, styled by an image model, reconstructed and split into separate meshes. It is not commercially available, and Meta says it is not ready for Horizon Worlds ([UploadVR](https://uploadvr.com/meta-worldgen-ai-generates-3d-worlds)). **SpAItial**, led by Matthias Niessner, raised a $13M seed for metric-scale editable scenes ([Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/synthesia-co-founder-raises-13m-to-build-the-holy-grail-of-ai-interactive-3d-worlds-from-text/)); its reported Echo-3 iPhone app and API rest on weak sourcing ([runtimewire](https://runtimewire.com/article/spaitial-echo-3-iphone-3d-world-capture)). Several products that look adjacent are not world generators. Roblox's 4D generation makes interactive objects only, with full scenes a stated future goal ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/04/robloxs-4d-creation-feature-is-now-available-in-open-beta/)). Meshy and Tripo remain single-asset tools ([SelfCAD](https://www.selfcad.com/blog/meshy-vs-tripo)). Epic's Unreal Engine 6 showed prompt-based scene generation and Claude Code building a city, but early access is targeted for around late 2027 ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/epic-reveals-unreal-engine-6-is-integrating-ai-models-so-developers-can-reduce-tedious-work/)).

## Open repos converged on panorama, video, then splats

The open-source lineage runs through three phases. In 2023–24, per-scene optimization repeatedly inpainted images and lifted them to 3D with depth estimates. In 2025, big labs released pipelines that lift a panorama or video into a mesh or 3DGS. By 2026 these had become multi-stage "world" pipelines: generate a panorama, plan a camera path, expand the scene with video or stereo models, then reconstruct it feed-forward into 3DGS. **HY-World 2.0, Matrix-3D, ABot-3DWorld 0 and, in non-panoramic form, Lyra 2.0 all use this recipe**, which makes it the de facto open architecture. ## Agents compose scenes by pairing LLM priors with geometric back ends

Intelligent scene generation has one recurring tension. LLMs know a lot about semantics, such as what belongs in a kitchen or which way a sofa faces, but their metric 3D grounding is poor, which shows up as collisions, floating objects and empty rooms. **Almost every system adds a geometric back end to compensate**: a constraint solver, differentiable optimization, a visual prior from an image model, or a physics simulation check. The field has moved from one-shot prompting (LayoutGPT, 2023), to LLM plus solver (Holodeck, I-Design, 2024), to VLM render-and-critique loops (SceneCraft, BlenderAlchemy, Scenethesis, SceneWeaver, 2025), to hierarchical multi-agent simulation-ready systems (SceneSmith, SAGE, 2026). In parallel, newer work trains policies to replace the slow critique loops.

Holodeck shows how the solver family works in practice. GPT-4 supplies spatial constraints, assets come from Objaverse via objathor, and a DFS solver places them (a MILP solver is optional). The result is AI2-THOR JSON loaded in Unity. Holodeck trained navigation agents for unseen scene types such as music rooms and daycares without human-built data, and it still needs an OpenAI key pinned to `gpt-4o-2024-05-13` ([GitHub](https://github.com/allenai/Holodeck)). That dependence on proprietary APIs runs through the whole field. Code-as-scene systems trade some realism for editability. **3D-GPT** (853 stars) has three agents drive Infinigen's procedural generators. **The Scene Language** represents a scene as program, words and embeddings, all inferred training-free ([arXiv 2410.16770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16770)). **SceneCraft** first builds a scene graph as a blueprint, writes Blender code, critiques renders with GPT-V, and accumulates reusable functions through "library learning", but no official code was found ([arXiv 2403.01248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01248)). Watch for the name clash: a different NeurIPS 2024 *SceneCraft* does layout-guided 3DGS rooms ([GitHub](https://github.com/OrangeSodahub/SceneCraft)). Some systems use LLM layouts to drive neural representations instead: GALA3D (307 stars) and GraphDreamer (201 stars) feed LLM-derived layouts or scene graphs into Gaussian splatting or SDS optimization ([GALA3D](https://github.com/VDIGPKU/GALA3D); [GraphDreamer](https://github.com/GGGHSL/GraphDreamer)). The learned non-LLM baselines that every paper compares against are ATISS, DiffuScene, CommonScenes, InstructScene and PhyScene ([ATISS](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/ATISS); [DiffuScene](https://github.com/tangjiapeng/DiffuScene); [CommonScenes](https://github.com/ymxlzgy/commonscenes); [InstructScene](https://github.com/chenguolin/InstructScene); [PhyScene](https://github.com/PhyScene/PhyScene)).

### Simulation-ready scene factories are the 2026 state of the art

Embodied AI drives the high end. The progression runs from ProcTHOR's 10,000 procedural houses ([GitHub](https://github.com/allenai/procthor)), through LLM-generated tasks and scenes in RoboGen (1,230 stars) and GenSim (360 stars) ([RoboGen](https://github.com/Genesis-Embodied-AI/RoboGen); [GenSim](https://github.com/liruiw/GenSim)), to RoboCasa's generative kitchens (1,855 stars, [GitHub](https://github.com/robocasa/robocasa)). **SceneSmith** (MIT/TRI, ICML 2026 Spotlight) builds in stages, architecture then furniture then small objects, with designer, critic and orchestrator agents at each stage. It reports **3–6× more objects than prior methods, under 2% inter-object collisions, 96% of objects stable in physics simulation, and 92% realism and 91% prompt-faithfulness win rates** in a 205-person user study ([arXiv 2602.09153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09153)). **NVIDIA SAGE** (CVPR 2026) generates task-conditioned simulation-ready scenes using TRELLIS assets and critics for semantics, realism and stability. It releases the **SAGE-10k** dataset, and policies trained only on its data generalize to unseen layouts ([arXiv 2602.10116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10116); [GitHub](https://github.com/NVlabs/sage)). Steerable Scene Generation, also from the SceneSmith group, does diffusion over SE(3) poses steered by RL and MCTS ([arXiv 2505.04831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04831)). **WorldClaw** scales the agentic pattern to open worlds: planning agents write a spec of regions, terrain, assets and materials, then the system builds a height-field terrain, reconstructs meshes and refines them with render-based agents ([arXiv 2608.05248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05248)). Others follow up at larger scales: multi-floor buildings (MANSION, [GitHub](https://github.com/AgibotGeneral/MANSION)), whole homes (HomeWorld), and cities (RAISECity, Yo'City, MajutsuCity at CVPR 2026), all catalogued in the [awesome list](https://github.com/hzxie/Awesome-3D-Scene-Generation).

Two counter-currents are worth following. Some authors argue that **image and diffusion models arrange space better than LLMs** and use them as the layout source: Architect, Scenethesis and SceneBloom ([arXiv 2411.09823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09823); [arXiv 2505.02836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02836); [arXiv 2610.32978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32978)). Others attack the cost of agent loops. SceneOrchestra says execute-review-reflect loops produce "suboptimal execution flows" and instead generates the full tool-call trajectory up front ([arXiv 2604.19907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19907)). FuncRoom-Agent replaces "costly test-time generate–evaluate–revise loops" with a learned DSL generator ([arXiv 2608.29519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29519)). Evaluation is the field's weakest point. **SceneEval** (WACV 2026 oral) found consistent failures on detailed constraints and physical plausibility across six methods ([arXiv 2503.14756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14756)). SceneCritic and SceneTeract show that VLM judges are unstable across viewpoints and miss geometry such as reach and clearance ([arXiv 2604.13035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13035); [arXiv 2603.29798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29798)). The training corpora are 3D-FRONT (18,968 rooms, [arXiv 2011.09127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09127)), InternScenes (about 40,000 simulatable scenes, [arXiv 2509.10813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10813)), SAGE-10k and SceneChain-12k. There is no shared leaderboard, so claims of state of the art are per-paper.

A plugin inside the editor opens a socket, and an MCP server exposes tools (create and transform objects, import assets, take screenshots) to Claude, Cursor or Codex. Two things stand out in the table. First, **the commercial company Aura (tryaura.dev) now owns or sponsors both the top Unity bridge and the flopperam Unreal bridge**, which points to early consolidation of the open bridges. Second, the most complete generate-and-place loop is still Blender's. blender-mcp lets an agent generate an asset with Hunyuan3D, Rodin, Meshy or Tripo, place it, and inspect a multi-angle render in one session. The open asset generators behind such loops are mature: **TRELLIS** (14,163 stars), **Hunyuan3D-2** (15,271 stars) and **TripoSR** (7,272 stars) ([TRELLIS](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS); [Hunyuan3D-2](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2); [TripoSR](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/TripoSR)). NVIDIA's 3D Object Generation Blueprint wires Llama 3.1 and TRELLIS into Blender, but it produces objects, not layouts ([GitHub](https://github.com/NVIDIA-AI-Blueprints/3d-object-generation)). No open tool was found that automatically generates and places assets inside Unity, Unreal or Godot, which is a clear gap. Research-grade agents keep appearing: InteriorAgent (3DV 2026, code pending), the Code4Scene benchmark for coding agents, and EZBlender's Plan-and-ReAct editor ([3DV 2026](https://www.computer.org/csdl/proceedings-article/3dv/2026/731200b436/2gPu1Aw1wbu); [arXiv Code4Scene](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36777v1); [arXiv EZBlender](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.07143)). BlenderGym is the benchmark for VLM Blender editing ([arXiv 2504.01786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01786)).

Minecraft has the most hobbyist "text to build" agents, and they take two routes. In the first, the LLM writes a validated build script: [minecraftlm](https://github.com/mattzh72/minecraftlm) and the [mcbuilder](https://github.com/lzif/mcbuilder) DSL. In the second, the LLM plans while bots or mods place blocks: [minecraft-agentic](https://github.com/NoblerWorks-HQ/minecraft-agentic) with four Mineflayer bots, the Forge mod [Steve](https://github.com/YuvDwi/Steve), and the MCP server [moling-minecraft](https://github.com/gojue/moling-minecraft). [APT](https://github.com/spearsheep/APT-Architectural-Planning-LLM-Agent) adds chain-of-thought blueprints, and [Word2Minecraft](https://github.com/JMZ-kk/Word2Minecraft) extends the 2D [Word2World](https://github.com/umair-nasir14/Word2World) story-to-level pipeline. [MarioGPT](https://github.com/shyamsn97/mario-gpt) (1,155 stars) remains the classic 2D LLM level generator. On the web, **no mature open project turns one prompt into a playable three.js world end to end**. The working pattern is to generate a world with Marble or an open model, render it with Spark, and have a coding agent add colliders and controls. Examples include [worldsplats](https://github.com/philchacko/worldsplats) (Spark, R3F and Rapier), the [Promptable World](https://github.com/Pran-Ker/Hackathon) hackathon build, World Labs' own [interactive examples](https://docs.worldlabs.ai/api/interactive-world-examples.md), and the open Marble alternative [WorldSplat](https://github.com/timscheuerai/worldsplat) (Wan 2.2 → MASt3R → gsplat). Others build a fixed engine that runs LLM output: [prompt-to-playable](https://github.com/bolajiev/prompt-to-playable) and [llm-game-engine](https://github.com/tomicz/llm-game-engine). CUHK's [OpenGame](https://github.com/leigest519/OpenGame) does agentic prompt-to-web-game generation with a 27B coder model. The most ambitious agent-built world found is [Athenaion](https://github.com/Leonxlnx/athenaion/wiki), a procedural ancient-Greek three.js world. Its self-reported figures: about 45k lines written by Claude Code across 55 subagents in about 45 hours.

The viral indie moments share a format: a short screen recording in which one prompt produces a space you can walk around in. **@levelsio's three-hour flight sim led directly to the 2025 Vibe Coding Game Jam**, which drew 1,170 games that had to be at least 80% AI-generated, judged by Andrej Karpathy and three.js creator Ricardo Cabello (@mrdoob) ([levels.io](https://levels.io/winners-of-the-2025-vibe-code-game-jam)). Its player numbers conflict across outlets, from nearly 2,000 at peak to 17,000 concurrent ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/307892/ai-gaming-massive-elon-musk-shares-game); [Avgeekery](https://avgeekery.com/new-flight-simulator-made-with-ai-earns-creator-5000-per-month/)). Indie work has since moved **from building generators to composing lab outputs**. Sidhu turned Genie frames into splats, Mason built a three.js game on a Marble world, and a Picsart workflow had Claude grey-box a scene in Blender before a video model rendered it photoreal ([Picsart](https://picsart.com/blog/create-with-blender-mcp-and-picsart-mcp-in-claude/)). The prolific posters are lab accounts (@GoogleDeepMind, @theworldlabs, @TencentHunyuan, @odysseyml), the curator @_akhaliq, and a small group of early testers and creators (@tejasdkulkarni, @bilawalsidhu). Individual author threads for WorldGen, WorldClaw and similar papers were not found, and no X posts were recovered for Mirage 2, Matrix-Game, SpAItial, Meta WorldGen or Odyssey-3.

The discussion around these demos splits into two camps. Enthusiasts praised Genie 3's lighting, multi-minute consistency and apparent non-rigid physics without an engine; its Hacker News thread drew 1,510 points ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44798166)). Marble drew "I'm floored", and engine developers welcomed its separate collider meshes ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45907541)). The critics were specific. Kulkarni called Genie 3 "far from being a real game engine": rigid-body stacking fails, 1v1 combat does not work, and combinatorial game logic breaks down. HN users measured about **1.1 s keypress-to-photon latency** ([X](https://x.com/tejasdkulkarni/status/1952737669894574264); [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44798166)). Marble's critics noted that quality degrades away from the input image, that demos are mostly on-rails camera moves, and that meshes are low quality ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45907541)). Blender artists said triangle rather than quad topology was a "dealbreaker" for hand refinement of MCP-built scenes ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44622374)). The split is substantive. Neural world models look like a future replacement for game engines, but production artists today value editability, parameter control and persistent state, which favors LLM-writes-code tools and asset-exporting generators.

## Conclusion

The three tracks are converging on one architecture without anyone announcing it. Matrix-Game 4.0 puts a neural renderer on top of a deterministic engine. Meta WorldGen and WorldClaw put an LLM planner in front of an image-to-3D reconstructor. SceneSmith and SAGE put VLM critics behind a physics simulator. Each combines a symbolic or engine layer that holds state, rules and collisions with a generative layer that supplies appearance, and that hybrid looks like the shape of a real "prompt-to-game" system. Pure pixel models such as Genie cannot be audited or saved, and pure LLM layout is metrically clumsy, so each track is borrowing the other's strengths. MCP bridges are where these meet in practice: an agent with blender-mcp or unity-mcp can already call Marble or HY-World for the environment, TRELLIS or Hunyuan3D for props, and the engine for physics. What is missing is an open orchestrator that does this reliably outside Blender.

For anyone building or investing, the open gaps are concrete. The field has no shared benchmark for either world generation or scene layout, so state-of-the-art claims are vendor claims. No open tool does generate-and-place natively in Unity, Unreal or Godot. Licenses (Tencent's community terms, WorldGrow's "TBD") and hardware (HY-Pano 2.0 at about 80B parameters) limit how open the "open" leaders really are. The AMD–World Labs deal and the wave of chip- and cloud-backed rounds suggest the commercial winners will be judged on how much compute their worlds consume as much as on how good they look, which pushes efficient, exportable geometry ahead of always-streaming pixels for anyone who has to pay per frame.
